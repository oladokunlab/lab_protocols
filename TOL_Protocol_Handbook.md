## 1.3 🦺 Lab Safety Commitments

Initial each statement.

- ____ 👟 **Closed-toed shoes and a lab coat** will always be worn in the lab.
- ____ 🧤 **Gloves and lab coats will not be worn outside the laboratory**, unless safety precludes any risk of contamination — such as moving autoclaved materials or biohazardous waste.
- ____ 🥽 **Safety goggles** will be worn when a splash hazard exists.
- ____ 🧯 **Fire extinguisher, spill kit, drench hose/eye wash, electrical shut-off and exits** will be identified and their operation understood.
- ____ 🔺 **Broken glass** will be disposed of in a broken glass container, never the regular trash.
- ____ 💉 **Sharps** (needles, scalpels) will be disposed of in a sharps container.
- ____ ♨️ **All biologicals** will be treated by autoclave (preferred) or chemical disinfection before leaving the building. Solid autoclaved waste will be crossed with an "X" of autoclave tape over the biohazard label, labeled as treated, bagged in an opaque trash bag, and placed in the outside dumpster.
- ____ 🧼 **Hands will be washed frequently.** Be aware of cross-contamination of surfaces; do not inadvertently transfer pathogens or ingest biologicals by touching your face or personal items. Personal items go in a marked "clean area" and are not used while working.
- ____ 🚨 **Laboratory injuries or possible laboratory-acquired infections** will be reported to the PI immediately.
- ____ 🧴 **All surfaces** will be cleaned and disinfected after use with 70% ethanol, 10% bleach, quaternary ammonia disinfectant, or 409®.
- ____ 🚧 **"Dirty" items** will be placed in their designated areas to prevent cross-contamination.
- ____ 🔥 **Flammable items** will be placed into the Flammable Cabinet when not in use.
- ____ 🧪 **Acids** will be separated in the Acid Cabinet, placed in tubs with absorbent material to contain spills.
- ____ 🏷️ **All chemicals and reagents** will be marked with received date and opened date, with the initials of the person receiving or opening the product.
- ____ ♻️ **TAMU-EHS procedures** will be followed for handling and disposing of chemicals and reagents.
- ____ 🔖 **All samples** will be identified by a simple code understandable by all. A paper copy of the code shall be placed in a waterproof bag within the sample box. Samples must include collection date and owner's initials. **All unlabeled samples will be disposed of by the PI as biohazardous waste.**
- ____ 💾 **All filenames** follow the format: *author last name, year of creation, short 3–5 word title, date.* Additions such as "rv by SO" may indicate who reviewed the document.
- ____ 📤 **Copies of data files** will be forwarded to the PI as they are produced — to keep everyone in the loop and to serve as backup.
- ____ 🗄️ **All electronic files will be backed up**: your computer, your personal backup drive, the PI's computer, and possibly a lab mate's computer. Paper copies of raw data are encouraged.

## 2.10 ⚗️ Reagent and Buffer Preparation

**Purpose** — To standardize reagent calculation, preparation, labeling and storage.

**Materials required**
- ⚖️ Analytical balance
- 💉 Calibrated pipettes
- 🧫 Volumetric glassware
- 💧 Deionized water
- 📏 pH meter
- 🔬 Sterile filter when required

**Procedure**
1. Review the formula and required final concentration.
2. Verify the calculation independently.
3. Record reagent names, grades, lot numbers and expiry dates.
4. Weigh or measure each component accurately.

6. Adjust pH at the required temperature when applicable.

9. Label with name, concentration, pH, date, expiry and preparer's initials.

**⚠️ Critical notes**
- Use the same buffer as the blank where buffer background may affect an assay.
- Do not use reagents showing contamination, precipitation or unexpected discoloration.

# Chapter 3 — 🧫 Primary Intestinal Cell Isolation & Culture

**Method: Cell Disaggregation and Isolation (Chicken Intestinal Epithelial Cells)**

*Modified from Athman et al. (2005), Guo et al. (2015), and Zaffuto et al. (2008)*

---

## 3.1 ⚗️ Materials and Reagents

### Enzymatic Digestion Solution — 25 mL per sample

| Component | Stock | Amount to Add | Final Concentration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 🧬 Collagenase Type I | Powder | 25 mg | 1 mg/mL |
| 🧬 Hyaluronidase | Powder | 2.5 mg | 0.1 mg/mL |
| 🧬 Dispase | Powder or liquid | 25 Units | 1 U/mL |
| 💧 HBSS (pH 7.4) | Sterile | Up to 25 mL | Base solution |

*Hanks' Balanced Salt Solution or PBS may serve as the base.*

**Preparation**
- 🧪 Add HBSS to a sterile 50 mL tube (~20 mL initially)
- ⚖️ Add Collagenase Type I (25 mg)
- ⚖️ Add Hyaluronidase (2.5 mg)
- ⚖️ Add Dispase (25 Units, or 25 µL)
- 🌀 Mix gently until dissolved
- 📏 Adjust volume to exactly 25 mL with HBSS
- 🔬 Filter through 0.22 µm filter for sterility
- ⏱️ **Prepare fresh before use**

### DMEM-W (Wash Medium) — no sorbitol available

**For 100 mL:**
- 🧴 DMEM base medium — **97.5 mL**
- 🐄 FBS — **2.5 mL (2.5%)**
- **Total: 100 mL**

Store at 4 °C; warm to 37 °C before use.

### Complete Culture Medium

**For 100 mL:**
- 🧴 DMEM base medium — **82 mL**
- 🐄 FBS — **15 mL (15%)**
- 💊 PSG — **1 mL (1%)**
- 💊 ITS — **1 mL (1%)**
- 💊 NEAA — **1 mL (1%)**
- **Total: 100 mL**

Store at 4 °C; warm to 37 °C before use.

---

## 3.2 🔪 Step 1 — Tissue Preparation

- 🐔 Collect fresh chicken intestinal tissue immediately after sacrifice
- 🚿 Wash tissue **3 times** with sterile PBS containing 1% Penicillin-Streptomycin
- ✂️ Open intestine longitudinally using sterile scissors
- 🧽 Gently scrape off excess mucus with a sterile glass slide or scalpel blade
- 🔪 Cut tissue into small **2–3 mm²** pieces using a sharp sterile scalpel blade
- 🧊 Keep tissue pieces in ice-cold sterile PBS until ready for digestion

---

## 3.3 🧬 Step 2 — Enzymatic Digestion

- 🧪 Transfer tissue pieces into a sterile 50 mL centrifuge tube containing **25 mL** pre-warmed (37 °C) enzymatic digestion solution
- 🔄 Place tube in an orbital shaker incubator
- ⚙️ Incubate with shaking:
  - 🌡️ **Temperature:** 37 °C
  - 🔄 **Speed:** 130 rpm
  - ⏱️ **Duration:** 30–60 minutes
- 🔍 Check digestion progress every 15 minutes by observing tissue dissociation
- 🛑 Stop digestion when tissue is well-dissociated by adding **5 mL ice-cold FBS**, or by transferring the tube to ice

---

## 3.4 🌀 Step 3 — Cell Collection (First Centrifugation)

- 🌀 Centrifuge at **300 × g for 5 min** at room temperature
- 💧 Carefully aspirate and discard supernatant (enzymes, debris, dead cells)
- ⚠️ Leave approximately **1 mL** of liquid to avoid disturbing the pellet

---

## 3.5 🚿 Step 4 — Cell Washing

- 💧 Add **10 mL sterile PBS** or HBSS to resuspend the pellet
- 🌀 Pipette gently up and down, or roll on the palm, to disperse cells — avoid bubbles
- 🌀 Centrifuge at **300 × g for 5 min** at room temperature
- 💧 Carefully aspirate supernatant
- 🔁 Repeat washing **2 more times** (3 washes total)
- 💧 After the final wash, aspirate supernatant carefully

---

## 3.6 ⚖️ Step 5 — Differential Centrifugation (Cell Purification)

- 🧴 Resuspend cell pellet in **10–15 mL DMEM-W** (DMEM + 2.5% FBS)
- **First differential spin:**
  - 🌀 Centrifuge at **50 × g for 3 min** at room temperature — removes large debris and tissue fragments
  - 📤 **Carefully transfer supernatant** (contains single cells) to a new sterile 50 mL tube
  - 🗑️ Leave the pellet behind (debris)
- **Second spin:**
  - 🌀 Centrifuge collected supernatant at **300 × g for 5 min** — pellets viable cells
  - 🧴 Aspirate supernatant and resuspend pellet in **10 mL fresh DMEM-W**
- 🔁 **Repeat differential centrifugation 3–4 times** until the supernatant after the 50 × g spin is clear
- 🌀 Centrifuge the clear supernatant at 300 × g, then aspirate carefully

---

## 3.7 🧴 Step 6 — Final Cell Resuspension

- 🧴 Resuspend the final cell pellet in **5–10 mL complete culture medium**:
  - DMEM + 15% FBS + 1% PSG + 1% ITS + 1% NEAA
- 🌀 Mix gently by pipetting up and down

---

## 3.8 🔢 Step 7 — Cell Counting and Viability Assessment

- 💉 Take **10 µL** of cell suspension
- 🔵 Mix with **10 µL of 0.4% Trypan Blue**
- 🔬 Load **10 µL** onto a hemocytometer
- 🔍 Count cells in 4 corner squares under the microscope (10× objective)

**Calculations**

> **Cell concentration (cells/mL) = (Average count per square) × 2 (dilution factor) × 10⁴**
>
> **Viability (%) = (Viable / unstained cells ÷ Total cells) × 100**
>
> **Total cells = Cell concentration (cells/mL) × Total volume (mL)**

✅ Acceptable viability should be **≥ 80%**

---

## 3.9 🌱 Step 8 — Initial Cell Seeding (Optimization)

**Prepare cell suspension at 1 × 10⁶ cells/mL.** *Example:* if you have 5 × 10⁶ total cells, add 5 mL complete medium.

**Seed three T25 flasks at different densities:**

| Flask | Cell Suspension | Complete Medium | Total | Cell Number |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 🧪 Flask 1 | 0.25 mL | 4.75 mL | 5 mL | 2.5 × 10⁵ cells |
| 🧪 Flask 2 | 0.5 mL | 4.5 mL | 5 mL | 5 × 10⁵ cells |
| 🧪 Flask 3 | 1.0 mL | 4.0 mL | 5 mL | 1 × 10⁶ cells |

**Incubation**
- 🔄 Swirl flasks gently to distribute cells evenly
- 🌡️ Place in incubator at **37 °C, 5% CO₂**, humidified atmosphere
- ⏸️ **Do not disturb for the first 24 hours** to allow cell attachment

---

## 3.10 🔍 Step 9 — Flask Selection (24 h after seeding)

- 🔬 Examine all three flasks under phase-contrast microscope
- Assess:
  - 📌 Cell attachment efficiency
  - 🧩 Cell spreading and morphology (epithelial cobblestone appearance)
  - 📈 Confluence level
  - ✨ Minimal debris
- ✅ Select the flask with best overall attachment and morphology; discard the others
- 🔄 Perform first medium change on the selected flask:
  - 💧 Aspirate old medium carefully
  - 🧴 Add 5 mL fresh pre-warmed complete medium
  - 🌡️ Return to incubator

---

## 3.11 🔄 Step 10 — Cell Maintenance

- 🧴 **Medium changes:** replace medium every **2–3 days**
  - Aspirate old medium; add 5 mL fresh pre-warmed complete medium
- 🔬 **Daily monitoring:** check cells under microscope for
  - 📈 Growth and confluence
  - 🧩 Morphology
  - ☣️ Contamination
- 🔀 **Passage cells** when they reach **80–90% confluence** (typically 3–5 days)

---

## 3.12 🔀 Step 11 — Cell Passaging (Subculture)

**When cells reach 80–90% confluence:**

1. 💧 Aspirate culture medium
2. 🚿 Wash with **5 mL sterile PBS**, swirl gently, aspirate (repeat once)
3. 🧪 Add **1 mL of 0.25% Trypsin-EDTA** to the T25 flask
4. 🌡️ Incubate at **37 °C for 3–5 min**
5. 👋 Tap flask gently to dislodge cells (check under microscope — cells should be rounded)
6. 🛑 Add **1 mL complete medium** to neutralize trypsin (total 2 mL)
7. 📤 Transfer cell suspension to a sterile 15 mL centrifuge tube
8. 🌀 Centrifuge at **300 × g for 5 min** at room temperature
9. 💧 Aspirate supernatant carefully
10. 🧴 Resuspend pellet in **5 mL complete medium**
11. 🔢 Count cells using the Trypan Blue method
12. 🌱 Seed new flasks or experimental plates at the desired density

---

## 3.13 📐 Step 12 — Standard Seeding Densities

| Flask / Plate | Surface Area | Cells to Seed | Medium Volume |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 🧪 T25 flask | 25 cm² | 2.5 – 5 × 10⁶ cells | 5 mL |
| 🧪 T75 flask | 75 cm² | 7.5 – 15 × 10⁶ cells | 15 mL |
| 🔲 24-well plate | 1.9 cm²/well | 2 – 4 × 10⁵ cells/well | 0.5 – 1 mL/well |
| 🔳 96-well plate | 0.32 cm²/well | 3 – 6 × 10⁴ cells/well | 100 – 200 µL/well |

🎯 **Target seeding density: 1 – 2 × 10⁵ cells/cm²**

---

## 3.14 🧭 Step 13 — Preparation for Experiments

**For experiments in 24-well or 96-well plates:**

- 🧴 **Optional pre-coating** (recommended for better attachment):
  - Prepare collagen I solution at 100 µg/mL in sterile PBS
  - 24-well plate: add 250–300 µL per well
  - 96-well plate: add 50–100 µL per well
  - 🌡️ Incubate 1 hour at 37 °C
  - 💧 Aspirate collagen solution before seeding cells
- 🌱 **Seed cells** at appropriate density (see table above)
- ⏱️ **Incubate 24 hours** at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ to allow attachment and equilibration
- 📈 **Check confluence:** cells should be 60–80% confluent before starting experiments
- ▶️ **Begin experiments** 24 hours after plating

---

## 3.15 📅 Expected Timeline

| Day | Activity | Expected Observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Day 0** | Tissue digestion and cell isolation | Cell suspension obtained |
| **Day 0** | Seed cells in T25 flasks (3 densities) | Cells in suspension |

**After enzymatic digestion**
- ✔️ Tissue well-dissociated with minimal large clumps
- ✔️ Cell suspension appears cloudy
- ✔️ Individual cells visible under microscope

**After cell counting**
- ✔️ Cell viability ≥ 80% (Trypan Blue exclusion)
- ✔️ Good yield (typically 1–5 × 10⁶ cells per gram of tissue)

**After 24 hours in culture**
- ✔️ ≥ 70% of cells attached to flask surface
- ✔️ Epithelial morphology (polygonal, cobblestone)
- ✔️ Minimal floating debris
- ✔️ Clear medium (pink color, stable pH)

**Before experiments**
- ✔️ Cells 60–80% confluent (not over-confluent)
- ✔️ Uniform monolayer formation
- ✔️ No signs of contamination
- ✔️ Proper epithelial morphology maintained

# Chapter 4 — 🔢 Cell Counting, Passaging & Seeding Protocol

**Purpose** — To determine viable cell concentration and seed equal numbers of cells into 96-well and 24-well plates.

---

## 4.1 🔬 Part 1 — Cell Counting (Hemocytometer)

1. 💉 Mix **10 µL** cell suspension with **10 µL** trypan blue.
2. 🔬 Load hemocytometer (10 µL).
3. 🔍 Count cells in the four corner squares.
4. 🧮 Calculate concentration:
   > **Cells/mL = Average count × 2 × 10,000**
5. ✅ Viability = (Live ÷ Total) × 100 — **must be > 90%**

---

## 4.2 🎯 Target Seeding Concentrations

| Plate | Cells per Well | Working Concentration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🔳 96-well | 25,000 cells/well | 250,000 cells/mL |
| 🔲 24-well | 150,000 cells/well | 300,000 cells/mL |

---

## 4.3 🧮 Worked Example — Stock = 1,200,000 cells/mL

### 🔳 96-Well Plate
- ➗ Dilution factor ≈ 1,200,000 ÷ 250,000 ≈ **5×**
- 🥣 Mix ratio: **1 part cells + 4 parts media**
- 📏 Total volume needed = (96 wells × 100 µL) + ~10% pipetting loss ≈ **11 mL**
- 🧫 Cells volume = Total ÷ Dilution factor = **2.2 mL**
- 🧴 Media volume = Total − Cells = **8.8 mL**
- 💉 Seed **100 µL per well**

### 🔲 24-Well Plate
- ➗ Dilution factor ≈ 1,200,000 ÷ 300,000 ≈ **4×**
- 🥣 Mix ratio: **1 part cells + 3 parts media**
- 📏 Total volume needed ≈ **13 mL**
- 🧫 Cells: **3.25 mL** · 🧴 Media: **9.75 mL**
- 💉 Seed **500 µL per well**

---

## 4.4 ⚡ Fast Mental Shortcut

**Purpose** — Rapidly determine dilution and prepare seeding suspension without full calculator-based calculations.

### 🧠 Memorize Target Concentrations
- 🔳 96-well: 25,000 cells/well → **250,000 cells/mL**
- 🔲 24-well: 150,000 cells/well → **300,000 cells/mL**

### Step 1 — ➗ Determine Dilution Factor
Divide stock concentration by target concentration.
*Example:* 1,200,000 ÷ 250,000 ≈ **5× dilution**

### Step 2 — 🥣 Convert to Mixing Ratio
**Dilution − 1 = media parts**

| Dilution | Ratio (cells : media) |
| --- | --- |
| 2× | 1 : 1 |
| 3× | 1 : 2 |
| 4× | 1 : 3 |
| 5× | 1 : 4 |

### Step 3 — 📏 Calculate Total Volume
- 🔳 96-well: wells × 0.1 mL
- 🔲 24-well: wells × 0.5 mL
- ➕ Add ~10% extra for pipetting loss

### Step 4 — ✂️ Split Volumes
- 🧫 Cells volume = Total ÷ dilution factor
- 🧴 Media volume = Total − cells volume

### 🧮 Example (Stock = 1,200,000 cells/mL)
- 🔳 96-well: ~11 mL total → **2.2 mL cells + 8.8 mL media**
- 🔲 24-well: ~13 mL total → **3.25 mL cells + 9.75 mL media**

---

## 4.5 🌱 Seeding Procedure

- 🌡️ Warm media to 37 °C
- 🧫 Make a single-cell suspension
- 🔄 **Remix frequently while pipetting** — cells settle fast
- 🎯 Pipette to the center of each well
- 🌀 Gently swirl the plate
- ⏸️ Incubate **undisturbed for 4–6 hours**
- 💉 Seeding volume: **96-well → 100 µL/well · 24-well → 500 µL/well**

# Chapter 5 — 🦠 Crude Extracellular Vesicle (EV) Isolation from *Clostridium perfringens*

**Polymer-Based Precipitation Method** using Total Exosome Isolation Reagent (Thermo Cat. # 4478359)

---

## 5.1 ⚗️ Materials & Reagents

- 🦠 *Clostridium perfringens* culture
- 🧴 Appropriate anaerobic broth medium
- 🌫️ Anaerobic culture system
- 💧 Sterile PBS (Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ free)
- 🔬 0.45 µm syringe filters
- 🔬 0.22 µm syringe filters
- 💉 Sterile syringes
- 🧪 Total Exosome Isolation Reagent (Thermo Cat. # 4478359)
- 🛡️ Protease inhibitor cocktail (optional)

## 5.2 🔧 Equipment

- 🌀 Refrigerated centrifuge
- ♨️ Heat block or water bath
- 🧊 Ice bucket
- 🧪 Sterile low-protein-binding tubes
- 💉 Pipettes and sterile tips

---

## 5.3 🧭 Procedure

### 🦠 Step 1 — Bacterial Culture Preparation
- Inoculate *Clostridium perfringens* into anaerobic broth medium
- 🌡️ Incubate anaerobically at **37 °C overnight (16–18 h)**

### 🌀 Step 2 — Removal of Bacterial Cells
- Transfer culture into centrifuge tubes
- 🌀 Centrifuge at **10,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C**
- 📤 Carefully transfer supernatant into new sterile tubes without disturbing the pellet

### 🔬 Step 3 — Filtration of Supernatant
- Filter supernatant first through a **0.45 µm** filter
- Filter again through a **0.22 µm** filter
- ✅ Collect sterile cell-free supernatant

### 🧪 Step 4 — Polymer-Based EV Precipitation
- ➕ Add **5 mL Total Exosome Isolation Reagent** to every **10 mL** filtered supernatant
- 🔄 Mix gently by inversion
- 🚫 **Do NOT vortex**

### ❄️ Step 5 — Incubation
- Incubate samples at **4 °C overnight (12–16 h)**

### 🌀 Step 6 — EV Pelleting
- 🌀 Centrifuge at **10,000 × g for 30 min at 4 °C**
- 💧 Carefully discard supernatant
- ⚠️ Pellet may appear small or invisible

### 🧴 Step 7 — EV Resuspension
- Resuspend pellet in **150 µL sterile PBS** (per every 5 mL Isolation Reagent + 10 mL supernatant)
- 🌀 Pipette gently up and down until fully resuspended
- 🛡️ **Optional:** resuspend the EV pellet in 150 µL sterile PBS containing 1× protease inhibitor. For a 100× protease inhibitor stock, mix **1.5 µL protease inhibitor cocktail with 148.5 µL sterile PBS**

### ♨️ Step 8 — Heat-Inactivated EV Control
- 🧪 Aliquot crude EV preparation into sterile microtubes
- 🌡️ Incubate at **65 °C for 30 min**
- 🧊 Immediately place on ice for **5 min**

### 🌀 Step 9 — Clarification
- 🌀 Centrifuge at **10,000 × g for 5 min**
- 📤 Transfer supernatant into a fresh sterile tube
- 🏷️ Label as: **Heat-inactivated crude EV**

---

## 5.4 🧊 Storage

- ❄️ **Short-term:** 4 °C (≤ 24 h)
- 🥶 **Long-term:** −80 °C
- 🚫 **Avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles**

## 5.5 ✅ Minimal Quality Control

- 🧪 Use **BCA assay** for protein quantification
- 🧫 Plate a small aliquot of the EV preparation on agar to **confirm sterility**

## 5.6 ⚖️ Experimental Controls

- 💧 PBS-only control
- 🦠 Crude EV preparation
- ♨️ Heat-inactivated crude EV
- 🧴 Precipitated sterile medium control
- 🧫 Unprecipitated culture supernatant

# Chapter 6 — 🧪 Bradford Assay Quantification of Crude EVs

**Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)**

This SOP describes the complete procedure for determining the protein concentration of crude extracellular vesicles using Thermo Scientific Bradford Protein Assay Reagent and Thermo Scientific Albumin Standard (Cat. #23209, 2 mg/mL).

---

## 6.1 🎯 Purpose

To quantify crude EV-associated protein concentration for normalization of antibacterial, MTT, ROS, and cytokine assays.

## 6.2 ⚗️ Materials and Reagents

- 🧪 Bradford Protein Assay Reagent
- 📏 Thermo Albumin Standard Cat. #23209 (2 mg/mL)
- 🦠 Crude EV samples suspended in PBS
- 💧 PBS
- 🔳 96-well flat-bottom microplate
- 🧫 Microcentrifuge tubes
- 💉 Single-channel or multichannel pipettes
- 📊 Plate reader capable of reading at **595 nm**

---

## 6.3 📏 Preparation of BSA Standards

Prepare **100 µL** of each standard as shown.

| Tube | Final Conc. (µg/mL) | BSA (2 mg/mL) | PBS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ⬜ Blank | 0 | 0 µL | 100 µL |
| 1️⃣ S1 | 125 | 6.25 µL | 93.75 µL |
| 2️⃣ S2 | 250 | 12.5 µL | 87.5 µL |
| 3️⃣ S3 | 500 | 25 µL | 75 µL |
| 4️⃣ S4 | 1000 | 50 µL | 50 µL |
| 5️⃣ S5 | 1500 | 75 µL | 25 µL |
| 6️⃣ S6 | 2000 | 100 µL | 0 µL |

---

## 6.4 🧫 EV Sample Preparation

- 🧊 Thaw EV samples **on ice**
- 🌀 Mix gently by pipetting — **do not vortex**
- 🧪 Prepare one **undiluted** sample
- ➗ Prepare a **1:5 dilution**: 20 µL EV + 80 µL PBS
- 🏷️ Label all tubes clearly

---

## 6.5 🔳 Plate Setup

Run all standards and samples in **triplicate**.

| Group | Rep 1 | Rep 2 | Rep 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blank | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 125 µg/mL | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 250 µg/mL | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 500 µg/mL | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 1000 µg/mL | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 1500 µg/mL | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 2000 µg/mL | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |

**For each well:**
- 💉 5 µL standard or EV sample
- 🧪 200 µL Bradford reagent
- 📏 **Total volume = 205 µL per well**

## 6.6 ⏱️ Incubation

Incubate the plate at room temperature for **10 minutes**, protected from direct light.

## 6.7 📊 Absorbance Measurement

Read absorbance at **595 nm** using a microplate reader.

## 6.8 📈 Data Analysis

- ➗ Average the triplicate absorbance values for each standard and sample
- 📉 Generate a standard curve — concentration versus absorbance
- 🧮 Determine EV protein concentration from the standard curve equation

---

## 6.9 🧮 Example Calculation

> Standard curve equation: **y = 0.0005x + 0.05**
> Measured EV absorbance = **0.65**
>
> 0.65 = 0.0005x + 0.05 → **x = 1200 µg/mL**
>
> If a 1:5 dilution was measured:
> **Actual EV concentration = 1200 × 5 = 6000 µg/mL = 6 mg/mL**

---

## 6.10 💉 Determining EV Treatment Volume

> **Example:** desired final concentration = 10 µg/mL in a 100 µL cell culture well.
>
> Protein required = 10 µg/mL × 0.1 mL = **1 µg**
>
> If EV stock = 1200 µg/mL:
> Required EV volume = 1 ÷ 1200 = **0.83 µL**

---

## 6.11 🎚️ Suggested EV Treatment Range

| Treatment Group | Final EV Concentration |
| --- | --- |
| 🟢 Low | 5 µg/mL |
| 🟡 Medium | 10 µg/mL |
| 🔴 High | 20 µg/mL |

---

## 6.12 📝 Notes

- ⬜ Always include a **PBS blank**
- 🧊 Keep EVs **on ice** during preparation
- 🚫 Avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles
- 🔁 Run standards and samples in **triplicate**
- ℹ️ Bradford measures **total crude EV-associated protein**

# 🅰️ Part A — Sample Characterization Protocols

## A1. 🧪 BCA Protein Assay for Crude CP-Sample Concentration

**Source/adaptation** — Adapted from Jiang et al. (2014), Nicholas et al. (2017), Choi et al. (2020) and Mei et al. (2020), who normalized bacterial samples/MVs by protein concentration using BCA-type assays.

**Purpose** — To determine test sample protein concentration so all experiments can be dosed at 1, 5 and 10 µg/mL.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🧊 Crude CP-sample stock on ice
- 🧪 BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo Scientific/Pierce or equivalent)
- 📏 BSA standards supplied with kit
- 💧 Sterile PBS or sample storage buffer
- 🔳 96-well clear flat-bottom plate
- 📊 Plate reader capable of reading **562 nm**
- 💉 Pipettes and sterile low-bind tubes

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Run all standards and samples in duplicate or triplicate
- Prepare at least two sample dilutions (e.g. 1:5 and 1:10 in PBS) so readings fall inside the standard curve

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🧊 Thaw sample aliquot on ice, mix by gentle pipetting, briefly spin down condensation
2. 📏 Prepare BSA standards per kit instructions — typical working range 0–2000 µg/mL
3. 💉 Add **25 µL** of each BSA standard into assigned wells
4. 💉 Add **25 µL** of diluted samples into assigned wells
5. 🧪 Prepare BCA working reagent per kit instructions
6. ➕ Add **200 µL** BCA working reagent to each well — final reaction volume **225 µL/well**
7. 🌀 Mix plate gently for 30 s without splashing
8. 🌡️ Incubate **30 min at 37 °C** or at room temperature
9. 📊 Read absorbance at **562 nm**
10. 🧮 Calculate sample concentration from the BSA standard curve and multiply by dilution factor

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- Report sample protein concentration as µg/µL and µg/mL
- Use this concentration to prepare the 10× working solutions in §7.3

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- If the sample buffer contains detergents or high protein background, use the **same buffer as the blank**
- BCA measures total protein and does not distinguish vesicle-associated from soluble protein contamination — interpret accordingly

---

## A2. 🧫 No-Live-CP / Sterility Check of Test Sample Preparation

**Source/adaptation** — Based on the sterility-check concept used by Nicholas et al. (2017), who streaked purified *C. difficile* MVs onto agar/media before storage/use.

**Purpose** — To confirm that test sample preparations do not contain viable CP before use on chicken cells.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🦠 Crude CP-sample aliquot (or any test sample)
- 💧 Sterile PBS / vehicle control
- 🧴 Mock-sample control
- 🧫 CP recovery/enrichment media and plates (BHI, SFP agar)
- 🌫️ Anaerobic incubation
- ⚖️ Appropriate positive and negative controls
- 🔁 **Test each sample batch before cell treatment**

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🌬️ In a biosafety cabinet / approved containment, test an aliquot of the sample preparation using the CP sterility method
2. ⚖️ Test the same volume of vehicle and mock-sample controls
3. ➕ Run the approved positive control separately to confirm the sterility test can recover viable CP
4. 💉 Take **10 µL** of the samples and plate on agar
5. 🌡️ Incubate **anaerobically at 37 °C for 24 hours**
6. 📝 Record presence/absence of growth per SOP acceptance criteria
7. ✅ Release sample batch for chicken-cell assays **only if no viable CP is detected**

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- ✅ **Pass:** no viable CP detected
- ❌ **Fail:** viable CP detected — do not use that lot for chicken-cell assays unless re-purified and re-tested

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- Keep a sterility record for **every** sample lot used in cell-culture experiments
- For publication, state that test samples were confirmed free of viable bacteria before cell exposure

## A3. 🧬 SDS-PAGE Protein Profile of Crude CP-Samples

**Source/adaptation** — Adapted from Jiang et al. (2014), Nicholas et al. (2017) and Mei et al. (2020), who compared MV/OMV protein profiles by SDS-PAGE and Coomassie staining.

**Purpose** — To compare the sample protein profile with whole-cell lysate and/or culture supernatant, and document sample lot consistency.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🦠 Crude CP-sample normalized by BCA
- 🧫 CP whole-cell lysate and CP culture supernatant protein comparator (prepared under approved SOPs)
- 🧪 4× Laemmli sample buffer with reducing agent
- 🧬 12% SDS-PAGE gel or 4–15% gradient gel (Bio-Rad or equivalent)
- 📏 Protein ladder
- 💧 Running buffer
- 🔵 Coomassie stain (e.g. PageBlue Protein Staining Solution, Thermo Scientific — used by Jiang et al. 2014 — or Bio-Rad Coomassie G-250)
- 📷 Gel imaging system

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Recommended loading: **10–20 µg protein/lane** for samples and whole-cell lysate (Jiang et al. loaded 20 µg of each)
- For concentrated supernatant comparator, load an equivalent protein amount or document the volume loaded

### 🧭 Procedure
1. ⚖️ Normalize all samples to the same protein concentration using BCA results
2. 🧪 For each sample, prepare **20 µL total loading volume**: sample containing 10–20 µg protein + 5 µL 4× Laemmli buffer + PBS to 20 µL
3. ♨️ Denature samples according to the gel system and lab SOP
4. 📏 Load molecular weight marker in lane 1
5. 💉 Load 10–20 µg sample protein, whole-cell lysate and culture supernatant protein in separate lanes
6. ⚡ Run SDS-PAGE until the dye front reaches the bottom of the gel
7. 🔵 Stain with Coomassie per reagent instructions
8. 💧 Destain if required and image the gel
9. 💾 Save image with lane labels and protein amount loaded

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- A distinct banding pattern supports a successful sample-enriched preparation
- Compare lot-to-lot banding consistency
- Use the same samples for western blot toxin screening if needed

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- SDS-PAGE alone does **not** prove vesicle purity — interpret with TEM, BCA and sterility data
- Avoid overloading test sample lanes, which obscures bands and increases background

## A4. 🎯 Western Blot or ELISA Screening for CP Toxins

**Source/adaptation** — Adapted from Jiang et al. (2014), who screened CP4 MVs for alpha toxin and NetB by western blot. Luo et al. (2020) showed CPB2 can directly injure epithelial cells, making CPB2 screening important if your strain carries *cpb2*.

**Purpose** — To determine whether test sample effects may be due to toxin-associated cargo or soluble toxin contamination.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🦠 Crude CP-samples, CP culture supernatant positive comparator and whole-cell lysate comparator (approved SOPs)
- ⚡ SDS-PAGE and transfer system
- 📄 PVDF or nitrocellulose membrane (Jiang et al. used nitrocellulose)
- 🏷️ Primary antibodies: anti-alpha toxin/anti-PlcC, anti-NetB, anti-CPB2 if available — choose validated antibodies for your strain/toxin
- 🔗 Secondary HRP- or AP-conjugated anti-rabbit or anti-mouse antibody
- 💡 Detection reagent: ECL for HRP, or BCIP/NBT solution (VWR) for AP — Jiang et al. used AP and BCIP/NBT
- 🥛 Blocking buffer: 5% non-fat milk in TBST, or vendor-recommended buffer
- 🧪 Optional ELISA kits for alpha toxin, NetB or CPB2 if commercially available

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Load **10–20 µg** sample protein per lane; include culture supernatant as positive comparator
- Run one membrane per toxin if antibody conditions differ

### 🧭 Procedure
1. ⚡ Separate samples by SDS-PAGE as in A3
2. 🔄 Transfer proteins to membrane per your transfer system
3. 🥛 Block with 5% non-fat milk in TBST for **1 h at room temperature**
4. 🏷️ Incubate with toxin-specific primary antibody at vendor-recommended dilution/incubation
5. 🚿 Wash **3× with TBST**, 5–10 min per wash
6. 🔗 Incubate with appropriate secondary antibody at vendor-recommended dilution
7. 🚿 Wash **3× with TBST**
8. 💡 Develop signal using ECL or BCIP/NBT depending on secondary antibody enzyme
9. 📝 Record whether toxin bands are present in sample, supernatant and lysate fractions

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- ✅ Toxin **not** detected in sample lane but detected in positive comparator → sample prep is less likely dominated by that toxin
- ⚠️ Toxin **detected** in sample lane → describe the preparation as a test sample/toxin-associated fraction and interpret cell effects cautiously

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- A negative western blot does not prove complete absence of toxin if antibody sensitivity is low
- If CPB2 is detected, include CPB2-only or toxin-neutralization controls if possible

## A5. 🧬 Sample-Associated DNA PCR Screen (Optional)

**Source/adaptation** — Adapted from Jiang et al. (2014), who tested CP MV-associated DNA for 16S rRNA, *plc*, *pfoA* and *netB*, and from Mei et al. (2020), who treated OMVs with DNase I before DNA extraction and 16S PCR.

**Purpose** — To determine whether crude CP-samples carry CP-associated DNA and toxin gene fragments.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🦠 Crude sample
- ✂️ DNase I (Thermo Fisher Scientific or equivalent; Mei et al. used 1 U/µL)
- 💧 PBS
- 🧬 Bacterial Genomic DNA Extraction Kit (Solarbio or equivalent)
- 🎯 PCR primers for 16S rRNA and strain-relevant genes: *plc*, *pfoA*, *netB*, *cpb2*, *cpe*
- 🧪 PCR master mix
- 🔄 Thermocycler
- ⚡ Agarose gel electrophoresis reagents

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Include: sample untreated with DNase, sample treated with DNase, CP genomic DNA positive control, and no-template control
- DNase pretreatment reduces external DNA contamination before sample disruption

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🧪 Aliquot sample into two tubes: DNase-treated and untreated control
2. ✂️ For the DNase-treated tube, add DNase I at your lab's or the manufacturer's concentration (Mei et al.: 1 U/µL, 10 min at 37 °C)
3. 🌀 Pellet or clean up samples per your lab protocol after DNase treatment
4. 🧬 Extract DNA using a bacterial genomic DNA extraction kit
5. 🎯 Set up PCR reactions for 16S rRNA and toxin/virulence genes relevant to your CP strain
6. ⚡ Run agarose gel and image PCR products
7. 📝 Record presence/absence of amplicons

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- 16S-positive sample DNA indicates CP-derived nucleic acid association
- *plc/pfoA/netB/cpb2/cpe* positivity supports toxin-gene DNA presence but does **not** prove toxin expression or biological activity

### ⚠️ Note — DNA PCR is supportive characterization only. Pair with toxin protein testing and functional assays.

## A6. 🔬 TEM Characterization of Crude CP-Samples

**Source/adaptation** — Adapted from Jiang et al. (2014), Nicholas et al. (2017), Choi et al. (2020) and Mei et al. (2020). Choi et al. diluted samples to 50 µg/mL, placed 10 µL on a 300-mesh copper grid and stained with 2% uranyl acetate for 5 min.

**Purpose** — To confirm sample morphology, estimate size range and assess aggregation/contamination.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🦠 Crude CP-sample
- 💧 Sterile filtered PBS
- 🔲 300- or 400-mesh carbon/Formvar-coated copper grids (EMS or equivalent)
- 🎨 2% uranyl acetate or EM-core-approved negative stain
- 🥢 Fine forceps
- 📄 Filter paper
- 🔬 TEM access (instrument depends on your core facility)
- 📦 Parafilm and clean grid box

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Prepare sample at **50 µg/mL** in PBS as used by Choi et al.; if too dense, also prepare 25 µg/mL
- Prepare PBS-only and mock-sample grids

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🧊 Thaw sample on ice and dilute to 50 µg/mL in sterile filtered PBS
2. 💧 Place a **10 µL droplet** onto the coated side of the grid
3. ⏱️ Allow adsorption for the time recommended by your EM core
4. 📄 Wick excess liquid gently with filter paper, without touching the center of the grid
5. 🎨 Apply **10 µL of 2% uranyl acetate**, stain up to **5 min** (Choi et al.) — or follow the EM core negative-staining SOP
6. 📄 Remove stain with filter paper and air dry completely
7. 📷 Image multiple random fields at suitable magnifications
8. 📏 Measure diameter of at least **50–100 vesicles** if image quality allows

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- Report morphology, size range, mean diameter ± SD and representative images
- CP MVs in Jiang et al. were approximately **20–400 nm**; *C. difficile* MVs (Nicholas et al.) and *L. paracasei* EVs (Choi et al.) were spherical vesicles

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- Crude preparations may show protein aggregates — TEM helps decide whether additional purification is needed
- Follow EM-core rules for stained grids containing pathogen-derived material

## A7. 📊 DLS or NTA Size Distribution

**Source/adaptation** — Adapted from Choi et al. (2020), who diluted purified samples to 1 µg/mL in PBS and measured size using a Zetasizer Nano ZS.

**Purpose** — To quantify particle-size distribution as a supplement to TEM.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🦠 Crude CP-sample
- 💧 Sterile filtered PBS
- 📊 DLS instrument (Malvern Zetasizer Nano ZS) or NTA instrument (NanoSight / ZetaView)
- 🧪 Low protein-binding tubes
- 🔲 Disposable cuvettes or NTA sample chamber materials

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Prepare dilution at **1 µg/mL** in filtered PBS for DLS (Choi et al.); prepare additional dilutions if the count rate is too high

### 🧭 Procedure
1. ➗ Dilute sample stock to 1 µg/mL in sterile filtered PBS
2. 🌀 Centrifuge briefly at low speed **only if needed** to remove large dust particles — avoid pelleting vesicles
3. 💉 Load sample into DLS cuvette or NTA chamber per instrument SOP
4. 🔁 Run at least **three technical measurements** per sample lot
5. 📝 Record hydrodynamic diameter and polydispersity index (DLS) or particle concentration (NTA)

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- Report modal/mean size, polydispersity and particle concentration if available
- Use TEM as the main morphology confirmation, because crude bacterial vesicle samples may be polydisperse

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- DLS is sensitive to large aggregates and may **overestimate** size in test samples
- NTA provides particle concentration but requires appropriate dilution and instrument access

## B1. 📈 CP Growth-Readout Assay with Sample Treatment

**Source/adaptation** — Conceptually based on bacterial vesicle functional assays in Jiang et al. (2014), Nicholas et al. (2017) and Mei et al. (2020), adapted here to test whether CP-derived test samples inhibit or promote CP growth. Live CP preparation must follow the lab SOP.

**Purpose** — To determine whether 1, 5 or 10 µg/mL test sample alters CP growth kinetics.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🦠 Standardized CP suspension prepared by the laboratory CP SOP
- 🧪 Crude CP-sample 10× working solutions: 10, 50 and 100 µg/mL
- 💧 PBS / sample vehicle
- 🧴 Mock-sample preparation
- ➕ Validated positive antibacterial control
- 🌫️ Anaerobe-compatible microplate, sealed plate system or approved tubes
- 📊 Plate reader for OD₆₀₀ (microplate format)
- 💉 Sterile pipette tips and reservoirs

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Recommended final volume: **1 mL/tube** or **200 µL/well**
- For 200 µL/well: 180 µL standardized CP suspension + 20 µL 10× sample/control
- For 1 mL/tube: 900 µL CP suspension + 100 µL 10× sample/control
- 🔁 Use at least **triplicate** wells/tubes per group

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🦠 Prepare the standardized CP suspension using your approved lab SOP
2. 🏷️ Label tubes/wells: CP only, vehicle, mock-sample, sample 1 / 5 / 10 µg/mL, positive control
3. 💉 Add CP suspension first — 180 µL per 200 µL well, or 900 µL per 1 mL tube
4. 💉 Add 10× sample or control — 20 µL per 200 µL well, or 100 µL per 1 mL tube
5. 🌀 Mix gently by pipetting or gentle plate shaking, without creating aerosols
6. 🌫️ Incubate using the laboratory anaerobic CP assay conditions
7. 📊 Measure OD₆₀₀ at baseline and selected time points (e.g. 0, 6, 12, 24 h)
8. 🧫 At endpoint, collect aliquots for viable-count confirmation using SOP B2

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- Plot OD₆₀₀ vs time
- **Percent growth inhibition = [(OD control − OD treated) / OD control] × 100**
- ✅ Confirm any OD-based effect by CFU, because sample particles can influence turbidity

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- If samples increase OD **without** increasing CFU, the apparent effect may be particle aggregation rather than bacterial growth
- If samples increase **both** OD and CFU, samples may **promote** CP growth rather than inhibit it

## B2. 🧫 Viable-Count Confirmation After Sample Treatment

**Source/adaptation** — Added as a required confirmation for OD-based antibacterial results; viability confirmation matters especially because test samples may affect turbidity.

**Purpose** — To determine whether sample treatment reduces viable CP burden.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🧪 Endpoint samples from B1
- 💧 Sterile diluent approved for CP enumeration
- 🧫 Laboratory CP plating/enumeration materials
- 🧪 Sterile tubes · 💉 Pipettes
- 🌫️ Approved anaerobic incubation system
- 🔢 Colony counter or manual counting sheet

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Use the same treatment groups as B1
- Use the dilution series, plating volume and incubation conditions already validated in your lab for CP enumeration

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🌀 At the selected endpoint, gently mix each sample
2. 💉 Remove a defined aliquot (e.g. 100 µL) for viable-count processing
3. ➗ Perform serial dilution using the laboratory-approved CP enumeration SOP
4. 🧫 Plate the validated volume from appropriate dilutions
5. 🌫️ Incubate under approved CP conditions
6. 🔢 Count colonies from countable dilution plates
7. 🧮 Calculate CFU/mL and log₁₀ CFU/mL for each sample

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- **Log reduction = log₁₀(CFU/mL control) − log₁₀(CFU/mL treated)**
- A decrease in log₁₀ CFU/mL supports antibacterial activity
- No CFU reduction but decreased toxin gene expression suggests **anti-virulence** rather than killing

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- 🚫 Do not report OD₆₀₀ alone as antibacterial activity
- Include the mock-sample control, because medium-derived carryover can influence bacterial growth

## B3. 🕳️ Agar Well Diffusion Assay

Added as a screening assay for antimicrobial activity of test samples against CP. Standardized CP suspension (**1.0 × 10⁶ concentration preferred**), recovery conditions and containment procedures must follow the laboratory SOP.

**Purpose** — To determine whether test samples produce a measurable zone of growth inhibition on an inoculated agar surface.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🦠 Standardized CP suspension per laboratory CP SOP (check McFarland SOP)
- 🧫 Laboratory-validated agar plates suitable for CP testing
- 🧹 Sterile swabs or validated plate-inoculation device
- 🕳️ Sterile 6 mm well borer or equivalent
- 🧪 Test-sample concentration series, including 1, 5 and 10 µg/mL where suitable
- ⚖️ Vehicle, mock-sample and validated positive antibacterial controls
- 💉 Sterile pipettes and tips
- 📏 Caliper or ruler for zone measurement
- 🌫️ Anaerobic incubation system

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Use the same standardized inoculum for all plates
- Use equal well diameter and equal sample volume for every treatment
- Run **at least three independent wells** per treatment
- Record both the sample concentration and total mass loaded per well

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🧹 Inoculate the complete agar surface uniformly with the standardized CP suspension (1.0 × 10⁶)
2. ⏱️ Allow excess surface moisture to absorb before making wells
3. 🕳️ Create wells of equal diameter using a cork borer (**4–9 mm**) and remove agar plugs carefully
4. 💉 Add an equal validated volume — commonly **50–100 µL** — of each test sample or control to the assigned well (depends on borer diameter)
5. ↔️ Keep wells sufficiently separated to prevent overlapping inhibition zones
6. ⏱️ Use the same pre-diffusion period for all plates
7. 🌫️ Incubate using laboratory CP assay conditions
8. 📏 Measure the **complete inhibition-zone diameter in mm, including the well diameter**
9. 📷 Record irregular, partial or indistinct zones; photograph plates with a scale where possible

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- Report mean inhibition-zone diameter ± SD for each treatment
- A larger clear zone indicates greater inhibition under the specific diffusion conditions used
- Compare treatments only when inoculum, agar depth, well size, sample volume and incubation conditions are identical

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- **A lack of a zone does not prove lack of antimicrobial activity** — large proteins, particles and hydrophobic materials diffuse poorly through agar
- Agar well diffusion is a **screening** method — confirm with MIC, MBC and/or CFU assays
- 🚫 Do not assign clinical susceptibility categories to nonstandard test samples

## B4. ⚪ Disk Diffusion Assay

**Source/adaptation** — Added as a standardized plate-based screening approach based on disk-diffusion principles. For experimental test samples, report **raw zone diameters** rather than clinical susceptibility categories.

**Purpose** — To screen whether a defined quantity of test sample inhibits CP growth around a sample-loaded disk.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🦠 Standardized CP suspension per laboratory CP SOP
- 🧫 Laboratory-validated agar plates
- ⚪ Sterile 6 mm blank paper disks or validated commercial disks
- 🥢 Sterile forceps or disk dispenser
- 🧪 Test-sample concentration series
- 💧 Vehicle-loaded blank disks
- ➕ Validated positive antibacterial control disk
- 📏 Caliper or ruler
- 🌫️ Anaerobic incubation system

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Use identical blank disks and loading volume for all experimental samples
- Report total mass of test sample loaded per disk
- Allow loaded disks to absorb the sample under sterile conditions before placement
- Use at least **three disks or independent plates** per treatment

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🧹 Inoculate the agar surface uniformly with the standardized CP suspension
2. ⏱️ Allow excess surface moisture to absorb
3. 💉 Load blank disks with the validated volume of test sample, vehicle or control
4. 🥢 Place disks on the inoculated agar using sterile forceps; press gently for complete contact
5. 🚫 **Do not move disks** after they contact the agar
6. ↔️ Space disks sufficiently to prevent overlapping zones
7. 🌫️ Incubate using laboratory CP assay conditions
8. 📏 Measure inhibition-zone diameter in mm
9. 📝 Record colonies within zones, double zones or indistinct edges separately

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- Report mean zone diameter ± SD and the amount of sample loaded per disk
- For experimental sample-loaded disks, report **raw zone diameters** without S/I/R classification
- Confirm important findings using MIC, MBC and CFU testing

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- Disk diffusion depends strongly on sample diffusion and may underestimate poorly soluble or particulate materials
- Use the same agar depth, inoculum, disk size, loading volume and incubation conditions across comparisons
- 🚫 Commercial antibiotic breakpoints must not be applied to nonstandard test-sample disks

## B5. 📉 Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC) Assay

Added as a broth-microdilution endpoint to quantify the lowest test-sample concentration preventing detectable CP growth under laboratory assay conditions.

**Purpose** — To determine the lowest concentration of test sample that inhibits detectable CP growth.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🦠 Standardized CP suspension per laboratory CP SOP
- 🧴 Laboratory-validated broth medium
- 🔳 Sterile clear 96-well microplate or validated assay tubes
- 🧪 Test-sample stock and serial dilution series
- ⚖️ Vehicle, mock-sample and positive antibacterial controls
- ⬜ Sample-only blank wells without CP
- 📊 Plate reader for OD₆₀₀ when optical measurement is used
- 💉 Sterile pipettes, tips and reservoirs

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Use a **two-fold serial dilution** bracketing the inhibitory endpoint whenever possible
- The 1, 5 and 10 µg/mL study concentrations may be included, but a broader series may be needed to establish a formal MIC
- For a 200 µL final well volume, one practical layout is **100 µL of 2× test sample + 100 µL standardized CP suspension**
- 🔁 Run at least **triplicate** wells per concentration

### 🧭 Procedure
1. ➗ Prepare the test-sample concentration series in the selected broth
2. 💉 Dispense sample dilutions into assigned wells
3. 🦠 Add standardized CP suspension to treatment, growth-control and vehicle-control wells
4. ⬜ Add sterile broth without CP to sample-only and sterility-control wells
5. ⚖️ Keep final volume and vehicle concentration equal in all wells
6. 🌀 Mix gently without producing bubbles or aerosols
7. 📊 Record baseline OD₆₀₀ if an optical endpoint is used
8. 🌫️ Incubate using laboratory CP assay conditions
9. 👁️ Assess growth visually and/or using the validated OD₆₀₀ endpoint
10. ➖ Subtract the matching sample-only blank from colored, turbid or particulate sample readings
11. 📝 Record the lowest concentration meeting the predefined no-growth criterion as the **MIC**
12. ✅ Confirm the MIC result by viable-count or MBC testing

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- Report MIC in µg/mL or the appropriate sample concentration unit
- If tested concentrations do not bracket complete inhibition, report as **> highest tested concentration** or as the lowest tested inhibitory concentration — do not assign an unsupported MIC
- Present replicate MIC values and the modal value across independent experiments

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- MIC indicates **inhibition**, not killing
- Test-sample color, turbidity or particles may interfere with OD — use sample-only blanks and viable-count confirmation
- Growth and sterility controls must perform as expected before the plate is interpreted

## B6. ☠️ Minimum Bactericidal Concentration (MBC) Assay

Added as a viable-recovery assay following MIC testing, to determine whether inhibitory concentrations are bactericidal.

**Purpose** — To identify the lowest test-sample concentration producing the laboratory-defined bactericidal endpoint.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🔳 Completed MIC plate or tubes
- 🧫 Laboratory-validated recovery agar
- 💧 Sterile diluent
- 💉 Sterile pipettes and tips
- 🌫️ Anaerobic incubation system
- 🔢 Colony-counting materials

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Use the MIC well and all higher concentrations showing no detectable growth
- Include the untreated or vehicle growth control and sterility control
- Use dilution, neutralization or washing where required to minimize test-sample carryover onto recovery agar

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🔍 Identify the MIC well and higher-concentration wells without detectable growth
2. 🌀 Mix each selected well gently
3. 💉 Remove a defined aliquot from each selected concentration
4. ➗ Dilute or neutralize the aliquot per the validated recovery method
5. 🧫 Plate the validated volume onto recovery agar
6. ⚖️ Process a growth-control sample in parallel
7. 🌫️ Incubate under laboratory CP recovery conditions
8. 🔢 Count recovered colonies
9. 📝 Determine the lowest concentration meeting the predefined bactericidal criterion
10. 📊 **Report MIC and MBC together**

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- A commonly used bactericidal endpoint is a reduction of **≥ 99.9%** relative to the starting or growth-control inoculum — but the exact criterion used must be stated
- Report MBC in the same units as MIC
- MBC/MIC ratio may be reported as a descriptive comparison

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- Residual test sample transferred to agar may suppress colony recovery and create a **false bactericidal result** — validate dilution or neutralization
- If colonies are recovered at every concentration, report that MBC was above the highest concentration tested
- Use CFU data rather than optical density to determine bactericidal activity

## B7. 🟢🔴 Bacterial Live/Dead Staining After Sample Treatment

**Source/adaptation** — Functional endpoint added to test membrane integrity; complements LDH/cytotoxicity concepts used by Jiang et al. (2014) and Luo et al. (2020).

**Purpose** — To visualize whether sample treatment damages CP bacterial membranes.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🧪 Sample-treated CP samples from B1
- 🟢 LIVE/DEAD BacLight Bacterial Viability Kit (Thermo Fisher/Invitrogen or equivalent)
- 💧 Sterile PBS
- ⬛ Black 96-well plate or microscope slides
- 🔬 Fluorescence microscope or flow cytometer
- ➕ Validated positive dead-cell control

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Use final sample doses **0, 1, 5 and 10 µg/mL**
- 🌑 Protect dyes from light. Use the bacterial staining kit exactly as directed by the manufacturer

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 💉 At endpoint, collect a small aliquot of each treatment sample under containment
2. 🚿 Wash or dilute samples in sterile PBS per kit instructions
3. 🎨 Prepare live/dead dye mixture per kit instructions
4. 🌀 Mix sample with dye mixture in the manufacturer-recommended volume ratio
5. 🌑 Incubate protected from light for the recommended time
6. 📤 Transfer stained sample to slide or black 96-well plate
7. 🔬 Image using green/red fluorescence channels, or analyze by flow cytometry
8. 📊 Quantify live/dead ratio using ImageJ, plate-reader fluorescence or cytometry gating

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- 🔴 Increased red/dead signal in sample-treated CP suggests **membrane damage**
- ➡️ No change in live/dead staining despite lower toxin gene expression suggests a **non-bactericidal anti-virulence effect**

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- Some anaerobes and dense samples may stain unevenly — optimize using positive live and dead controls
- Check sample autofluorescence/background using sample-only dye controls

## B8. 🟣 CP Biofilm Inhibition / Disruption Assay (Crystal Violet Readout)

**Source/adaptation** — Added to evaluate sample effects on CP attachment/biomass. Not directly from one uploaded paper, but complements the vesicle functional analysis framework used by Jiang et al. (2014), Nicholas et al. (2017) and Mei et al. (2020).

**Purpose** — To test whether crude CP-samples prevent biofilm formation or disrupt established CP biofilm.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🦠 Standardized CP suspension per approved SOP
- 🧪 Crude sample 10× working solutions
- 💧 PBS/vehicle and mock-sample
- 🔳 Sterile flat-bottom 96-well plate compatible with biofilm assay
- 🟣 Crystal violet solution (commonly 0.1% w/v or lab-validated concentration)
- 💧 PBS
- 🧪 Solubilization solution: 30% acetic acid or lab-validated alternative
- 📊 Plate reader for **570–590 nm**
- ☣️ Approved disinfectant/waste system for CP biofilm material

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- **Biofilm inhibition format:** add sample at time zero
- **Biofilm disruption format:** establish biofilm per approved SOP, gently wash, then add sample treatments
- Suggested final volume **200 µL/well**: 180 µL CP suspension/medium + 20 µL 10× sample/control

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🔳 Prepare treatment plate with at least **triplicate** wells per group
2. 🦠 For inhibition assay, add 180 µL standardized CP suspension to each well
3. 💉 Add 20 µL 10× sample/control to reach 0, 1, 5 or 10 µg/mL final concentration
4. 🌫️ Incubate using the approved CP biofilm SOP
5. 💧 At endpoint, remove planktonic suspension carefully without disturbing attached biofilm
6. 🚿 Wash wells gently with PBS using the lab-approved containment method
7. 🧪 Fix biofilm if required by your SOP before staining
8. 🟣 Add **200 µL crystal violet** per well and incubate anaerobically
9. 🚿 Remove stain and wash gently until background is low
10. 🧪 Add **200 µL 30% acetic acid** (or validated solubilization solution) per well
11. 📤 Transfer 100–150 µL solubilized stain to a clean plate if needed
12. 📊 Read absorbance at **570–590 nm**

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- **Biofilm biomass (%) = (OD treated / OD CP control) × 100**
- Reduced crystal violet staining suggests reduced attached biomass
- ✅ Pair with biofilm CFU or SEM — crystal violet does **not** measure viability

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- Crystal violet is sensitive to washing technique — use consistent gentle washing
- If the strain forms weak biofilms, this assay may require optimization by your lab

## B9. 🧬 CP Virulence / Toxin Gene-Expression qRT-PCR After Sample Treatment

**Source/adaptation** — Gene-target selection based on Jiang et al. (2014), who examined CP MV-associated 16S rRNA, *plc*, *pfoA* and *netB*, and Luo et al. (2020), who measured inflammatory/apoptosis/tight-junction gene expression by qRT-PCR using the 2^−ΔΔCt method.

**Purpose** — To determine whether test samples suppress or increase CP virulence gene expression independent of bacterial killing.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🧪 Sample-treated CP samples from B1
- 🧬 Bacterial RNA extraction kit suitable for **Gram-positive** bacteria
- ✂️ DNase treatment reagent
- 🔄 cDNA synthesis kit (e.g. PrimeScript RT reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser, Takara — used by Luo et al. for cell RNA)
- 🧪 SYBR qPCR master mix (e.g. SYBR Premix Ex Taq, Takara, or Applied Biosystems SYBR Green qPCR Mastermix)
- 🎯 Primers for CP genes: 16S rRNA / *rpoA* / *gyrA* housekeeping; *plc*, *netB*, *cpb2*, *pfoA*, *cpe* if strain-relevant
- 🔬 Real-time PCR system

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Harvest RNA at a time point **before major death occurs**, or normalize carefully to CFU
- Use **three biological replicates** and **technical qPCR triplicates**

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🧫 Collect treated bacterial samples using the laboratory CP handling SOP
2. 💥 Pellet/lyse samples using the bacterial RNA kit instructions validated for Gram-positive bacteria
3. ✂️ Perform DNase treatment to remove genomic DNA
4. 📏 Quantify RNA and assess purity
5. 🔄 Reverse transcribe **equal RNA input per sample** (e.g. 500 ng – 1 µg RNA depending on kit and yield)
6. 🧪 Prepare **20 µL qPCR reaction** (Luo et al. style): 10 µL 2× SYBR mix + 0.8 µL forward/reverse primer mix (or 0.4 µL each primer) + 2 µL cDNA + nuclease-free water to 20 µL. Adjust primer volume to your stock concentration
7. 🔬 Run qPCR using validated primer annealing conditions
8. 📊 Analyze relative expression using **2^−ΔΔCt**

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- Report fold change vs CP-only or vehicle control
- ⬇️ Reduced *plc/netB/cpb2/pfoA/cpe* expression suggests an **anti-virulence effect**
- ⬆️ Increased expression suggests samples may stimulate CP stress response or virulence signaling

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- Use RNA extraction methods that effectively lyse Gram-positive CP
- Interpret gene expression **with CFU data** — lower biomass may reduce apparent transcript abundance

## C1. ☠️ Sample-Only Cytotoxicity Screen in Chicken Cells

**Source/adaptation** — Adapted from Choi et al. (2020), who tested *L. paracasei* EV cell viability at doses including 1 and 10 µg/mL, and from Luo et al. (2020), who used MTT to evaluate epithelial-cell viability.

**Purpose** — To identify whether crude CP-samples are toxic to chicken cells before challenge experiments.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🐔 Chicken intestinal epithelial cells or HD11 chicken macrophage cells
- 🧴 Complete cell culture medium appropriate for the cell type
- 🧪 Crude CP-sample 10× working solutions
- 💧 PBS/vehicle and mock-sample
- 🔳 96-well clear plate
- 🎨 Cell viability reagent: CCK-8/WST-8 kit (DoGen Bio Cell Viability Assay Kit as used by Choi et al.), MTT reagent (Solarbio, used by Luo et al.), or Promega CellTiter 96/CellTiter-Glo
- 📊 Plate reader — **450 nm** for CCK-8/WST-8, **570 nm** for MTT

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Suggested seeding density: one giving **70–90% confluence at treatment endpoint**; for many epithelial cells, start with **1 × 10⁴ cells/well in 100 µL** (Luo et al. used 1 × 10⁴ IPEC-J2 cells/well for MTT)
- Final treatment volume: **100 µL/well**

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🌱 Seed 100 µL cell suspension per well in a 96-well plate; allow cells to attach overnight
2. 🧪 Prepare 10× sample working solutions at 10, 50 and 100 µg/mL
3. 🧴 Remove old medium carefully if required and add **90 µL fresh medium** per well
4. 💉 Add **10 µL** PBS/vehicle, mock-sample or 10× sample solution per well → 0, 1, 5 or 10 µg/mL final
5. ⏱️ Incubate for **12 h and/or 24 h** (Choi et al. used 12 h EV treatment for viability; Luo et al. used 24 h toxin treatment)
6. 🎨 Add viability reagent per kit instructions. For CCK-8/WST-8: add 10 µL reagent to 100 µL culture medium per well and incubate until color develops (Choi et al. incubated 3 h, read at 450 nm)
7. 📊 Read absorbance at the kit-recommended wavelength
8. 🧮 Calculate viability relative to untreated cells

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- **Cell viability (%) = (Abs treated / Abs untreated control) × 100**
- ✅ Proceed with doses showing acceptable sample-only viability, ideally **≥ 90%**
- ⚠️ Interpret doses **< 80%** as cytotoxic

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- Include blank wells containing **medium + reagent without cells**
- Use phenol red/serum conditions compatible with your selected viability kit

## C2. 🛡️ Cell Viability Protection Assay After CP Challenge

**Source/adaptation** — Adapted from Luo et al. (2020) for MTT viability, and Choi et al. (2020) for EV pretreatment before inflammatory challenge.

**Purpose** — To test whether crude CP-sample pretreatment improves chicken-cell viability after CP challenge.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🐔 Chicken intestinal epithelial cells
- 🧪 Crude sample 10× working solutions
- ⚔️ Sterile CP challenge material prepared by laboratory SOP — preferably filtered CP supernatant/toxin fraction for first-round studies
- 💧 PBS/vehicle and mock-sample
- 🔳 96-well clear plate
- 🎨 MTT, CCK-8 or WST-8 viability kit
- 🧪 DMSO if using MTT (Luo et al. dissolved formazan in 100 µL DMSO per well)
- 📊 Plate reader

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Final sample pretreatment volume: **100 µL/well**
- Use sample pretreatment for **12 h** (Choi et al. style design), then challenge for **12–24 h**
- 🚫 Do not include antibiotics during live CP challenge unless your approved SOP requires otherwise; for sterile CP supernatant challenge, maintain lab-validated cell culture conditions

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🌱 Seed cells in 96-well plate (e.g. 1 × 10⁴ cells/well in 100 µL) and culture overnight
2. 🧪 Prepare treatment medium: **90 µL fresh medium + 10 µL 10× sample/control** per well → 1, 5 or 10 µg/mL
3. ⏱️ Pretreat cells with sample/control for **12 h**
4. ⚔️ Add CP challenge material using the determined volume and concentration (**MOI of 1**). Keep final volume consistent across wells
5. ⚖️ Include challenge-only wells and no-challenge sample-only wells
6. ⏱️ Incubate **12–24 h** depending on injury kinetics
7. 🎨 **CCK-8/WST-8:** add 10 µL reagent per 100 µL culture volume, incubate per kit, read **450 nm**
8. 🎨 **MTT (Luo et al.):** add 10 µL MTT per well, incubate **4 h**, remove medium carefully, add **100 µL DMSO** per well, incubate **10 min protected from light**, read **570 nm**
9. 🧮 Calculate percent viability relative to untreated control

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- ✅ Protection is supported if CP challenge lowers viability and sample pretreatment **restores** viability
- 📊 Plot viability (%) for untreated, challenge-only, sample-only and sample + challenge groups

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- 🎯 Run an **injury pilot first** to choose a CP challenge condition that reduces viability without killing nearly all cells — a **30–50% reduction** is useful for detecting protection
- If test samples alone are toxic, protection results may be difficult to interpret

## C3. 💧 LDH Release Assay for Cytotoxicity / Membrane Damage

**Source/adaptation** — Adapted from Luo et al. (2020), who seeded 1 × 10⁵ cells/well in 24-well plates, treated for 24 h, collected supernatant and read LDH at 450 nm using a Jiancheng kit. Jiang et al. (2014) also used a Biovision LDH kit for MV cytotoxicity.

**Purpose** — To determine whether sample pretreatment reduces CP-induced cell membrane damage.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🐔 Chicken intestinal epithelial cells
- 🔲 24-well plate
- 🧪 Sample 10× working solutions
- ⚔️ CP challenge material per approved SOP
- 🎨 LDH Cytotoxicity Assay Kit: Jiancheng (Nanjing, China), Biovision, or Promega CytoTox 96
- 📊 Plate reader capable of 450 nm or kit-specified wavelength
- 💧 Sterile PBS and complete medium
- ➕ Positive lysis control supplied with kit

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Suggested seeding density: **1 × 10⁵ cells/well in 500 µL – 1 mL** medium in 24-well plates (adapted from Luo et al.)
- Treatment volume: **1 mL/well** is convenient — 900 µL medium + 100 µL 10× sample/control

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🌱 Seed 1 × 10⁵ cells/well in a 24-well plate; incubate overnight
2. 🧪 Prepare 10× sample/control treatments
3. 🧴 Replace medium with **900 µL fresh medium** per well
4. 💉 Add **100 µL 10× sample/control** → 0, 1, 5 or 10 µg/mL
5. ⏱️ Pretreat cells for **12 h**
6. ⚔️ Add CP challenge material per the pilot injury condition; keep final volume equal in all wells
7. ⏱️ Incubate **12–24 h**
8. 📤 Collect culture supernatant into labeled tubes; clarify gently if kit recommends
9. 💉 Transfer the kit-specified volume of supernatant into a 96-well assay plate — commonly **50–100 µL/well**
10. 🎨 Add LDH reaction mixture per kit instructions
11. 🌑 Incubate protected from light per kit instructions
12. 📊 Read absorbance at 450 nm or kit-specified wavelength

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
> **LDH release (%) = [(experimental LDH − spontaneous LDH) / (maximum LDH − spontaneous LDH)] × 100**
> *(when a maximum lysis control is included)*

✅ Protection is supported if challenge increases LDH and sample pretreatment reduces LDH release.

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- Avoid disturbing cells before collecting supernatant
- If using live CP challenge, ensure supernatants are handled/inactivated per biosafety SOP before plate-reader work

## C4. 🔥 DCFDA ROS Assay for Oxidative Stress

**Source/adaptation** — Adapted from Luo et al. (2020), who used 10 µM DCFH-DA, 1 mL/well in 6-well plates, incubated 20 min at 37 °C and read fluorescence at Ex/Em 488/525 nm. User-selected kit: Abcam DCFDA/H2DCFDA Cellular ROS Assay Kit.

**Purpose** — To test whether sample pretreatment reduces CP-induced intracellular ROS in chicken cells.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🐔 Chicken intestinal epithelial cells
- 🔲 6-well plate for microscopy/flow, or ⬛ black 96-well plate for plate-reader quantification
- 🎨 Abcam DCFDA/H2DCFDA Cellular ROS Assay Kit or Solarbio DCFH-DA
- 🧴 Serum-free medium or kit assay buffer
- 🧪 Sample 10× working solutions
- ⚔️ CP challenge material per approved SOP
- ➕ Positive ROS control supplied/recommended by kit (e.g. tert-butyl hydroperoxide if lab-approved)
- 🔬 Fluorescence plate reader or microscope with FITC/GFP channel — **Ex/Em ≈ 488/525 nm**

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- **6-well format:** seed 1 × 10⁵ cells/well in 2 mL medium (as in Luo et al. for ROS experiments)
- **96-well quantification:** seed optimized density, typically 1–2 × 10⁴ cells/well in 100 µL

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🌱 Seed cells and allow attachment overnight
2. ⏱️ Pretreat with samples at 1, 5 and 10 µg/mL for **12 h** using 10× additions
3. ⚔️ Challenge cells with CP challenge material for the selected injury time
4. 💧 Remove treatment medium carefully
5. 🚿 Wash cells once with PBS or assay buffer
6. 🎨 Prepare DCFDA working solution per Abcam kit. Following Luo et al., use final **10 µM DCFH-DA**
7. 💉 6-well plate: add **1 mL** of 10 µM DCFDA working solution per well. 96-well plate: add **100 µL** per well
8. 🌑 Incubate **20 min at 37 °C protected from light** (Luo et al.); use kit instructions if different
9. 🚿 Wash cells 1–2× with 100 µL serum-free medium or assay buffer to remove extracellular dye
10. 📊 Read fluorescence at **Ex/Em 488/525 nm**, or image with a fluorescence microscope
11. ⚖️ Normalize fluorescence to viable cell number if CP challenge strongly changes cell density

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- **ROS fold change = fluorescence treated / fluorescence untreated control**
- ✅ Protection is supported if CP challenge increases ROS and sample pretreatment decreases ROS

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- DCFDA is **light-sensitive** and can auto-oxidize — prepare fresh working solution
- Include sample-only + DCFDA wells to check sample-related fluorescence/background

## C6. 🔬 Annexin V-FITC / PI Apoptosis Assay

**Source/adaptation** — Adapted from Luo et al. (2020), who used an Annexin V-FITC/PI kit from Beyotime; cells were resuspended in 195 µL binding buffer and stained with 5 µL Annexin V-FITC plus 10 µL PI for 20 min in the dark.

## C7. 🧬 qRT-PCR for Inflammatory, Oxidative-Stress, Barrier and Apoptosis Genes

**Source/adaptation** — Adapted from Luo et al. (2020), Choi et al. (2020) and Mei et al. (2020). Luo et al. used TRIzol, PrimeScript RT reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser, SYBR Premix Ex Taq and 20 µL qPCR reactions. Mei et al. used HD11 cells and measured chicken IL-1β, IL-6, IL-2, iNOS and IL-10.

**Purpose** — To define whether sample pretreatment reduces inflammation/oxidative stress and preserves barrier and survival pathways.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🐔 Chicken intestinal epithelial cells or HD11 cells
- 🔲 6-well plate
- 🧪 TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen or TransGen Biotech — Luo used TransGen; Choi/Mei used Invitrogen)
- 🔄 cDNA kit: PrimeScript RT reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (Takara) or RevertAid First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Thermo Scientific)
- 🧪 SYBR Premix Ex Taq (Takara) or Applied Biosystems SYBR Green qPCR Mastermix
- 🎯 Primers for chicken genes of interest
- 💧 Nuclease-free water
- 🔬 Real-time PCR instrument (e.g. ABI ViiA7, QuantStudio, Roche LightCycler)
- 📏 NanoDrop or equivalent

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Suggested seeding: 6-well plates at a density reaching **70–90% confluence at harvest**
- **Harvest time:** 6–12 h for cytokine/inflammatory response; 12–24 h for oxidative/barrier/apoptosis genes
- **Recommended genes:** IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8/CXCLi2, TNF-α, IL-10, TGF-β, iNOS, Nrf2, HO-1, NQO1, SOD1, SOD2, CAT, GPx, ZO-1, OCLN, CLDN1/CLDN3, Bax, Bcl-2, caspase-3, GAPDH or β-actin

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🌱 Seed cells in 6-well plates; allow attachment overnight
2. ⏱️ Pretreat cells with samples at 1, 5 and 10 µg/mL for **12 h**
3. ⚔️ Challenge with sterile CP challenge material for the selected period
4. 🚿 Remove medium and wash cells once with PBS
5. 🧪 Add **1 mL TRIzol** per well of a 6-well plate, or the volume recommended by the RNA kit
6. 🧬 Extract total RNA per reagent instructions
7. 📏 Quantify RNA and assess purity using **A260/A280**
8. 🔄 Reverse transcribe equal RNA input per sample — Luo et al. used **1 µg total RNA**
9. 🧪 Prepare **20 µL qPCR reaction** (Luo et al.): 10 µL 2× SYBR Premix Ex Taq + 0.4 µL forward primer + 0.4 µL reverse primer + 2 µL cDNA + 7.2 µL nuclease-free water. *(With a combined primer mix: 0.8 µL primer mix + 6.4 µL water.)*
10. 🔬 Run qPCR using primer-validated cycling conditions. Mei et al. used: **95 °C 10 min → 30 cycles of (95 °C 30 s, 60 °C 1 min, 72 °C 1 min) → final extension 72 °C 8 min**
11. 📊 Analyze relative expression by **2^−ΔΔCt** using GAPDH or β-actin as housekeeping control

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
**Protective pattern:**
- ⬇️ Lower IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8/CXCLi2, TNF-α and iNOS
- ⬆️ Higher IL-10 / TGF-β
- ⬆️ Higher antioxidant genes
- 🛡️ Preserved ZO-1 / OCLN / claudins
- ⬇️ Lower Bax / caspase-3 and ⬆️ higher Bcl-2

Report fold change vs untreated **and** vs CP challenge-only group.

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- Validate chicken primer specificity and efficiency before the main experiment
- Do not compare gene expression without checking RNA quality and **no-RT controls**

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- Use **chicken-specific kits** — human/mouse cytokine kits often do not cross-react with chicken proteins
- For HD11 macrophages, Mei et al. supports IL-1β, IL-6, IL-2, iNOS and IL-10 as relevant endpoints

# 🅳 Part D — TEM and SEM Imaging Protocols

## D1. 🔬 TEM Image Acquisition and Sample Size Analysis

**Source/adaptation** — Based mainly on Choi et al. (2020) — 10 µL of 50 µg/mL sample on 300-mesh copper grids with 2% uranyl acetate for 5 min — and supported by Jiang et al. (2014), Nicholas et al. (2017) and Mei et al. (2020).

**Purpose** — To generate publishable images of CP-samples and quantify vesicle diameter distribution.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🔲 TEM grids prepared in A6
- 🔬 TEM microscope and EM-core imaging software
- 📏 ImageJ/Fiji for size measurement
- 📐 Calibration scale from TEM images

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- Image sample-only, PBS blank and mock-sample grids
- Use at least **three independent sample preparations** if possible

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🔍 Acquire low-magnification images to assess distribution and aggregation
2. 🔎 Acquire higher-magnification images showing vesicle membrane morphology
3. 💾 Save images with **scale bars**
4. 📏 Open images in ImageJ/Fiji and set scale using the scale bar
5. 📐 Measure diameter of **50–100 clearly defined vesicles** per sample lot
6. 📤 Export measurements to Excel/GraphPad
7. 📊 Plot size distribution histogram; report mean ± SD or median with IQR

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- 📷 Representative TEM image panel
- 📏 Sample size range and distribution
- 🔍 Qualitative purity/aggregation assessment

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- 🚫 Do not measure overlapped aggregates as single vesicles
- Report negative-stain method and grid type in methods

## D2. 🔬 SEM of Sample-Treated CP Cells

**Source/adaptation** — SEM added as an imaging endpoint to support antibacterial/biofilm results; TEM-based MV morphology and bacterial characterization were used by Jiang et al. (2014), Nicholas et al. (2017), Choi et al. (2020) and Mei et al. (2020).

**Purpose** — To visualize CP surface morphology after treatment with 1, 5 and 10 µg/mL sample.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🦠 Sample-treated CP samples from B1, or biofilm samples from B8
- 🧪 SEM-grade fixative approved by EM core (commonly glutaraldehyde-based)
- 💧 PBS or cacodylate buffer per EM core SOP
- 🧴 Ethanol dehydration series
- 💨 Critical point dryer or HMDS drying system
- 🔩 SEM stubs and conductive carbon tape
- ✨ Sputter coater with gold/palladium or equivalent
- 🔬 SEM access

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- **Groups:** CP only · vehicle · mock-sample · sample 1 · sample 5 · sample 10 · positive antibacterial control
- ☣️ All pathogen samples must be **chemically fixed/inactivated** per EM-core and biosafety approval before removal from containment

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🧪 Treat CP samples with sample/control using approved CP assay conditions
2. 🧫 At endpoint, collect cells or biofilm-coated coverslips using approved containment procedures
3. 🧊 Add EM-core-approved fixative in the approved ratio/volume; fix for the approved time
4. ✅ Confirm sample release requirements with the EM core before transport
5. 🚿 Wash fixed samples with buffer per EM SOP
6. 🧴 Dehydrate through graded ethanol series per EM SOP
7. 💨 Dry samples using critical point drying or HMDS method
8. 🔩 Mount on SEM stubs using conductive carbon tape
9. ✨ Sputter coat with conductive metal
10. 📷 Image multiple random fields at low and high magnification

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
Look for surface roughening, shrinkage, collapse, perforation-like damage, debris, aggregation or biofilm-matrix disruption. Interpret SEM **together with CFU and live/dead data**.

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- SEM shows **morphology, not viability**
- Avoid claiming membrane rupture unless supported by multiple imaging fields and viability/permeability data

## D3. 🔬 SEM of Chicken-Cell Morphology After CP Challenge

**Source/adaptation** — Built from the epithelial injury logic in Luo et al. (2020), who observed morphological damage, viability loss, LDH release, ROS increase and tight-junction disruption after CPB2 exposure.

**Purpose** — To visualize whether sample pretreatment preserves chicken epithelial-cell morphology after CP challenge.

### ⚗️ Materials and Reagents
- 🐔 Chicken intestinal epithelial cells grown on sterile coverslips
- 🔲 24-well plate
- 🧪 Sample working solutions
- ⚔️ Sterile CP challenge material or approved live challenge model
- 🧊 SEM fixative and processing reagents per EM core
- 🔩 SEM stubs, conductive tape and sputter coating supplies
- 🔬 SEM access

### 🗺️ Assay Setup
- **Groups:** untreated · CP challenge only · sample 10 µg/mL only · sample 1 + challenge · sample 5 + challenge · sample 10 + challenge
- 🔗 Use parallel wells for viability/LDH/ROS to connect morphology to quantitative injury data

### 🧭 Procedure
1. 🌱 Seed cells on coverslips; allow monolayer formation
2. ⏱️ Pretreat cells with samples for **12 h** at 1, 5 and 10 µg/mL
3. ⚔️ Apply CP challenge material for the selected injury time
4. 🚿 Wash gently with PBS if allowed by the model and EM core protocol
5. 🧊 Fix coverslips with EM-core-approved fixative
6. 🧴 Process through dehydration and drying per EM core SOP
7. 🔩 Mount coverslips on SEM stubs and sputter coat
8. 📷 Image multiple random fields per coverslip
9. 📊 Score morphology using a predefined scale, or quantify intact monolayer area if possible

### 📈 Readout and Analysis
- ⚔️ CP challenge should show cell rounding, detachment, surface damage or monolayer disruption if the injury model works
- ✅ Sample protection is supported by preserved monolayer and cell surface structure

### ⚠️ Notes / Critical Points
- SEM is strongest when **paired with MTT/CCK-8, LDH, ROS and tight-junction immunofluorescence**
- Use identical microscope settings across groups when comparing morphology

# 📊 Data Reporting and Statistics

- 📏 Report all sample doses as **µg/mL based on BCA protein concentration**, and state that samples were crude unless further purified
- 📈 For each endpoint, report **mean ± SD or SEM** from at least **three independent experiments**
- 🧮 Use **one-way ANOVA** with Dunnett or Tukey post-hoc tests for multiple groups, or **two-way ANOVA** when comparing sample dose and challenge status
- 🧬 For qPCR, report **2^−ΔΔCt fold change** and include housekeeping gene validation
- 📷 For microscopy, include representative images **plus** quantitative analysis where possible — size distribution, fluorescence intensity, junction continuity, biofilm area or morphological scoring
- ⚠️ **Avoid claiming sample-specific causality** unless mock-sample, sterility and toxin-screen controls support it
